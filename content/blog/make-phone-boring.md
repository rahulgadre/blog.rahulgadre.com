---
title: "Make the Phone Boring"
date: 2026-11-22 09:30:00 -07:00
tags: ["Habits", "Productivity"]
description: "Breaking bad phone habits by making the phone boring."
---

While checking my weekly screen time stats earlier this year, I realized I was spending an awful lot of time on my phone, mostly scrolling through social media. It wasn't adding much value to my life. Most of the time was spent watching meaningless reels that didn't really add any value. I also felt like I was losing a lot of time and didn't have enough time to do the work that mattered. At that moment, I decided to change things around and reclaim the time I was wasting on my phone.

From my experience, there is no easy way to break a habit. Keeping the phone in another room or not looking at it for some time doesn't really work, as the moment we get our hands on the phone, we end up spending a lot of time on it without keeping track of time. Therefore, some tough decisions need to be made to break a bad habit. As James Clear mentioned in his book *Atomic Habits*, making a habit difficult to engage in can help you lose interest in it. The phone wasn't the issue here; the issue was the apps that were taking up too much of my time. I then deleted my social media profiles and got rid of the apps. It's not like I was going to miss much by not accessing those apps.

After removing the social media apps, I saw a massive change in my screen time stats. I managed to reclaim some time, but there was still more room for improvement. I was at a point where I couldn't delete some of the other apps that were taking up my time. I then enabled app limits in the screen time section of my phone. I set aggressive limits on the apps I couldn't get rid of and made sure I stuck to them. This allowed me to reclaim even more time.

One other thing I did to make the phone boring was turn on grayscale mode, which turns your phone black and white and makes it less appealing. After making these three changes, I started using my phone only when required, and my screen time stats confirmed that. Three hours saved in a day equals 21 hours in a week and 1,092 hours in a year. Now, that's a lot of time to get back from something that's not going to add much value. I hope this helps others cut down on their phone usage and reclaim the valuable time they're losing.

In a nutshell, make the phone boring enough that you stop reaching for it every chance you get.
