## Why I Picked It Up

I had this book on my shelf for quite some time but never got around to reading it. After finishing *The Correspondent*, I decided to pick this one up next. Like the previous book, it's a page-turner with modern, conversational language. I also wanted to see real examples of focusing on yourself and letting go of the need to please others — and since it already had great reviews online, I went for it.

## What It's About

Mark Manson's core argument is simple: we only have a limited number of things we can care about, so we'd better choose wisely. Instead of chasing positivity and trying to please everyone, the book pushes you to accept discomfort, own your values, and stop worrying about what others think. Focus on what *you* think is best for you.

## Key Takeaways

Stop caring about what others say and invest your energy in what genuinely matters to you. The book is a no-nonsense reminder to stop trying to care about everything and instead focus on what truly deserves your attention. Accepting your limitations actually frees you — stop trying to please everyone, because someone is always going to be unhappy with your actions regardless. Being wrong is part of growth, not something to avoid. It's okay to be wrong, but the key is to learn from it and move forward.

## What I Liked

I mainly enjoyed the stories — particularly the Bukowski one and the story about a Japanese soldier who kept fighting long after the war was over. The messages that followed each story were powerful, and the occasional humor made the book engaging throughout.

## Final Thoughts

Overall, an enjoyable read. The book does a good job of reminding you that you shouldn't worry too much about what others say and instead focus on what you think is best for you. It's not groundbreaking philosophy, but it's packaged in a way that's direct, entertaining, and easy to internalize.

**My rating: 7.5 / 10**
